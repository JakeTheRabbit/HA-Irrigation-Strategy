# Validated feature matrix

This table separates what is implemented in the source, what the automated checks prove, and what has been verified on a live installation. Each release's audit, attached to its GitHub release as `release-audit-v<version>.md`, records what was run for that release and what was not.

Physical water delivery, a completely blank HA installation and a complete live grow-plan boundary handoff remain uncommissioned. Use the [user guide](USER_GUIDE.md) for daily workflows and [installation guide](INSTALL.md) for prerequisites.

The rows follow the order of the project story: what it watches, how it decides, how it stays safe, what it does, how you see it, how you change it, the AI layer, how you know it is working, and how to start. The website tells the same story with pictures: [cropsteering.growlabs.nz/features](https://cropsteering.growlabs.nz/features).

## Some facts to know

- The answers of the AI assistant are judgements, not guarantees. The log records each answer with its confidence and the action of the code.
- The controller calculates the water totals from the settings and the valve time. They are not a measurement.
- The dashed lines of the rest of the day are projections. The controller always waters by the probe.
- The controller waters by VWC and EC. It does not control the climate.
- An update keeps your rooms, settings, counters, plans and the history of the AI assistant.

## Improvements that are now practical

- Plan every zone across the grow without rewriting daily setpoints by hand.
- See what a steering change means before saving, including actual hydraulic assumptions.
- Use one curve for planning and a separate combined history for measured behavior.
- Keep room/zone identities stable as a facility changes.
- Detect unsupported/stale control snapshots and setup adoption gaps instead of displaying false success.
- Check dripper output locally and review the proposed correction before changing configuration.

## In progress, not in a release yet

These are built or being built on the parity branches (`feat/parity-backend`, `feat/parity-system`) and are not in any release. They get rows above only when they ship.

- **Shot timeline:** every shot in every room on one timeline (done, planned, failed, waiting for the pump), with sensor-driven future shots drawn as expected, not certain.
- **Timer programs:** water mothers, clones and zones without probes on a timer, with the next 24 hours drawn before saving. Timed shots pass the same safety checks.
- **Pump queue:** pump output and zones at once; a shot waits its turn when the pump is busy.
- **Feed probe calibration and dosing pump prime and calibration:** step by step, applied as a corrected reading or a measured flow, and read back.
- **Probe offsets and curves:** applied before a zone's probes are combined.
- **Journal, put back and undo:** every change with who and why; put any earlier value back.
- **Presets and copy:** stored on the controller, with a preview of only the values that change.
- **Alert rules, alerts inbox and quiet hours:** rules as sentences tested against yesterday, open alerts in one place, quiet hours per phone and a multi-phone test.
- **System status:** what is reporting, whether the controller is running, and what has gone silent.
- **Display units and wall dashboards:** EC in mS/cm or PPM, temperature in °C or °F; a Home Assistant dashboard from ready-made cards.
- **MCP previews:** an AI client prepares timers, alert rules, presets, copies and probe offsets as previews that a person applies.

## Valuable next additions

Measured-flow reconciliation is the strongest next step: compare commanded litres with independent water-meter deltas and identify blocked/leaking lines. Next, align recorded shot events with VWC/EC history, then quantify planned versus observed dryback over repeated days beyond the available visual comparisons. Reliable measurements would support proposal-only recipe adjustments and cultivar-specific profiles. Those features require clean real observations and have not been claimed as implemented here.
