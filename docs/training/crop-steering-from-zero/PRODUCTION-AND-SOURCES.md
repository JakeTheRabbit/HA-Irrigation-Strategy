# Production, evaluation and evidence

Prepared 19 October 2026. **Original production rationale and evidence ledger.** The current [Academy guide](../../ACADEMY.md) describes the delivered 24-lesson website, including five recorded pilots and 19 narrated slide lessons. Production intentions below can describe an earlier proposed filming approach. Human listening review, audience outcome trials and YouTube uploads have not been completed or claimed.

## Editorial and visual rules

- Friendly adult language. Explain an unfamiliar word before relying on it. Never joke about a learner being stupid, and never use intoxication as a framing device for operating equipment.
- Film one question at a time. Introduce water alone, then EC, then reference, then controls. Remove irrelevant panels from explanatory graphics; use the real full interface for navigation/handover.
- Use blue VWC and pink EC where matching the interface, plus direct labels, line styles and units. Label actual, saved reference and draft independently. Do not rely on colour alone.
- Never make actual measured data animate to follow a target change. Do not draw a smooth trace across missing observations without a clear gap/interpolation label. Day/night reference continuity must not imply a validated physiological forecast.
- Put “Example only — not recommended crop settings” beside numeric examples. Do not use facility targets as generic teaching defaults.
- Separate physical demonstration, screen recording, conceptual animation and synthetic data. Label time compression and illustrative animations.
- Target concept lessons around 4–7 minutes, procedure lessons around 6–9; split at a meaningful decision if longer. These are editorial defaults. Comprehension and complete checks take priority over a timer.
- Prefer a real calm presenter, real bench objects, legible screen capture and simple vector diagrams. No decorative sci-fi dashboards or AI-generated measurement screenshots. Synthetic chart data must be labelled.
- Record clean voice around a comfortable conversational pace; remove music under explanations and calculations. Leave pauses with explicit “Pause and try” cards.

## Accessibility and resources

Provide corrected captions, a downloadable transcript describing meaningful visual actions, chapter timestamps, the workbook and a glossary beside every playlist. Say values and units aloud. Describe a moved threshold rather than saying only “this line.” Check text and controls on a phone; zoom capture without hiding the selected room or the review scope. Quiz feedback explains the error and allows a retry. Provide accessible text/table equivalents of diagrams.

Do not equate different presentation formats with “visual learner” or “auditory learner” personality categories. Offer alternatives because they improve access and allow review.

## Record the pilot, then test understanding

Default production owner: the project maintainer or a nominated educator. Builder can choose recording tools, presenter, edit software, thumbnail style and release dates without changing the learning goals. Use a human presenter/voice by default; do not generate paid media or imitate a person without a separate production decision.

1. Record A1/A2/A3 from the supplied scripts. Use spare medium and synthetic graphics; no live writes.
2. Recruit 3–5 genuine novices by ordinary consent. Do not contact people automatically. Ask what they think VWC means before viewing and record their answer without ridicule.
3. Watch each learner use the material. Ask the workbook questions after each lesson. Observe hesitation, wrong assumptions and whether they can explain the idea without copying the sentence.
4. Give a different numerical example and a mislabelled/stale-sensor scenario. Check transfer rather than recognition of the exact example.
5. Revisit the core questions after approximately a week when feasible. Report sample size, tasks and results honestly; this is a formative pilot, not a controlled efficacy trial.
6. Rewrite the explanation wherever the same misconception recurs. Retest with a fresh novice before recording the whole catalogue.

Proposed release checks: every learner who will operate live equipment must demonstrate scope/isolation/failure-response tasks correctly with local supervision; ordinary concept checks should be achievable without coaching after revision. For production-quality evaluation, aim for at least 4 of 5 pilot learners explaining each key concept and solving a new example correctly. With 3–4 participants report actual counts, not an inflated precision percentage. These are proposed acceptance criteria, not established psychometric thresholds.

YouTube views, watch time and comments may help discover confusing sections. They do not establish competence, crop benefit or safe operation. Compare pre/post/delayed task results and practical error rates instead.

## YouTube packaging

Course name: **Crop Steering from Zero**. Use four named playlists and episode IDs in descriptions/website index. Lead titles with the question a beginner has, then add the course name if space permits. Thumbnail: one real object or graph, one short phrase, consistent lesson identifier. Avoid yield guarantees and aggressive stress claims.

Description template:

```text
In this lesson you will be able to: [observable outcome].
Start here: [previous/prerequisite lesson].
Practise: [matching workbook section].
Equipment/reference sheet: [course resource URL once published].
Software version shown: [verified version and capture date].
Example numbers are illustrations, not recommended crop targets.
Chapters: [actual edit timestamps].
Sources and corrected transcript: [lesson resource URL once published].
Next lesson: [published URL, or clearly state not yet published].
```

These are templates, not functioning published course links. For the initial trailer: show a pot, a probe, the graph, then a learner correctly previewing a change; promise understanding and a tested setup, not a yield outcome.

Before upload, review the actual video, thumbnail, audience setting, description and outgoing links against current YouTube policies. Educational context is not a blanket exception to regulated-goods restrictions. Keep the series focused on legitimate horticultural measurement and safe equipment operation; do not include drug-sale facilitation. No guarantee of availability, age classification or monetisation is made. ## Update and release procedure

For each course release, keep a manifest: lesson ID, recording date, software/firmware versions, demo seed/session, source-check date, asset origin, reviewer and known limitations. Use synthetic public captures. Never embed access tokens in the manifest.

Recheck before recording/uploading: current navigation and labels; draft/apply/readback; active-plan ownership; save/arm/lights-on timing; selected-room control scope; mapping acknowledgement; measurement units and type; graph/reference boundaries. A changed workflow requires replacement capture and a transcript note. A cosmetic-only change may use a dated correction card if it remains unambiguous.

Publish transcripts/workbook beside videos only after the production batch is reviewed and hosting paths are real. Fix errors with visible correction notes; update the matching workbook answer, not just a pinned comment. Do not call a revised lesson validated until the relevant learner task has been checked again.
