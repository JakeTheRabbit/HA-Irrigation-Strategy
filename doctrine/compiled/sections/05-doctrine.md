## Doctrine (id [evidence] text; sources)
Evidence: research > vendor > grower-practice for agronomy; product-rule always wins on what the software does.
### Core concepts
CON-01 [grower-practice] Three controls on every substrate: VWC is the position, dryback is the main steering lever, pore EC is the second dial. The substrate changes the numbers, not the method. (GL-LAW, GL-ROCKWOOL)
CON-02 [grower-practice] Generative steering means a deeper dryback, higher pore EC, fewer irrigation events with less runoff, an earlier last shot and a longer overnight dryback. Vegetative is the reverse. Sources disagree on whether generative shots should be larger or smaller, so treat shot size as an EC and runoff lever rather than a generative or vegetative one. (GL-LAW, GL-COCO, SKILL-CONCEPTS, VND-IRRIGATION)
CON-03 [product-rule] A grow-day is one photoperiod, anchored to lights-on. Daily water and shot counters reset when the zone moves into P0 at lights-on (or at its first decision of a new grow-day if the light edge was missed), never at midnight. (ENGINE, CTRL)
CON-04 [research] VWC is the share of substrate volume that is liquid water. Bulk EC is the conductivity of the whole wet substrate; pore EC, the salt the roots feel, is estimated from bulk EC, VWC and temperature. (GL-PROBE, HILHORST2000)
CON-05 [grower-practice] Steering is a bias built over days, never an overnight switch: move a target one notch, watch a full cycle, then decide the next notch. (GL-LAW, GL-COCO)
### P1 ramp
RMP-01 [grower-practice] P1 refills the substrate after the morning dryback in a series of shots and climbs to the zone's own full mark for the day. Once there it gives one more shot, and keeps going only while each shot still lifts the day's peak; when a shot no longer does, the substrate is full and P1 hands over to P2. (GL-LAW)
RMP-02 [vendor] Refill with several small shots, about 2-6% of substrate volume each (commonly 3-5%), 15-30 minutes apart. One large shot runs past a dry core instead of wetting it. (VND-IRRIGATION, GL-SLAB)
RMP-04 [product-rule] Near full, each shot's lasting rise shrinks. The engine ends P1 when a shot at or above the ceiling raises the day's peak by less than half a point, or at the maximum shot count. There is no time-based exit, and pore EC does not hold P1 open: P2 dilution corrects it. The ramp call may still hand over sooner when the curve already shows the slab full (ACT-03). (ENGINE, GL-PROBE)
RMP-05 [grower-practice] The full mark (field capacity) belongs to this zone: learn it from about five agreeing waterings and expect it to shrink as roots fill the substrate. Judge 'full' against the zone's own recent peaks. With Auto Setpoints on, code sets field capacity to the highest reading of this grow-day or the one before instead (CT-12), so it is only as good as the probe and the zone reads at or over it at every rising peak. (GL-PROBE, GL-LAW, CTRL)
RMP-06 [grower-practice] If P1 never reads full, suspect a target above the peak the substrate can hold (compare the detected Vmax), a blocked dripper, a wrong flow or substrate configuration, or too few ramp shots, before adding water. (GL-SLAB, SKILL-PLAYBOOK, ENGINE)
RMP-07 [grower-practice] Runoff appearing while moisture barely rises means channeling, a dry core or poor block contact. More volume does not fix it; inspect and rewet slowly. (GL-SLAB, GL-LAW)
RMP-08 [vendor] Let plants transpire for roughly 30 minutes to 2 hours after lights-on before the first shot. The extra fall after lights-on (vendor guidance: about 1-5% of the peak, relative) is planned, not a missed shot. It is the drop P0's dryback_target is compared with: a target near it lets P0 end on the plants' own drinking, while a larger one leaves P0 to its timeout, the bypass (VWC already at the P2 threshold) or the dawn call. (VND-IRRIGATION, OSS-CTRL, GL-SLAB, ENGINE)
RMP-09 [grower-practice] A vegetative day starts the ramp early with a short climb to a high peak; a generative day starts later after a longer overnight dryback and peaks lower in the band. (GL-ROCKWOOL)
RMP-10 [vendor] Vendor guidance has the P1 shots supply all the water early in the crop, with P2 maintenance shots added only once the relative daily dryback passes roughly 25 % of peak. The engine cannot do this: P1 always hands over to P2, and P2 tops up whenever VWC falls below the P2 threshold, with no P2 switch and no gate on the daily dryback. For young plants the nearest lever is a lower P2 threshold (fewer, later top-ups and a bigger daily dryback); the brain's later-rewater notch moves it at most 2 points under its current value (ACT-07; with Auto Setpoints on that value also shifts with field capacity), further is the grower's setting, and the controller keeps that threshold at least 3 points above the rescue floor, so it never reaches the vendor's P2-off state. Never propose turning P2 off or lowering the rescue floor to get there. (VND-IRRIGATION, VND-COCO, ENGINE, CTRL)
### Dryback
DRY-01 [research] Dryback depth, chiefly the overnight dryback, is the master lever: deeper steers generative, shallower steers vegetative. A mild water deficit raises abscisic acid, which shifts the plant toward flower and resin. (GL-ROCKWOOL, GL-LAW, WELLING2025, CAPLAN2019)
DRY-02 [product-rule] Units and meaning: relative dryback = points / peak x 100 (a 15-point fall from a 60 peak is 25% relative). The engine's dryback_target is relative and is compared in two places only: P0 ends once the drop since lights-on reaches it (the controller restarts the peak at the lights-on reading), and in the last 3 hours before lights-off P2 hands over early when reaching it from the day's peak, at the current drying rate, needs between the hours left to lights-on and 12 hours. It does not set the overnight dryback, and a large value (the integration's defaults are 40-50) leaves P0 to its timeout and switches the predictive P3 off. Arc and substrate dryback bands describe the overnight dryback: compare them with the zone's observed overnight dryback as code converts it, and never treat them as values for dryback_target. Point bands (true water content) and relative bands (vendor) are not interchangeable; use the converted figures code supplies. (ENGINE, CTRL, INTEG, VND-IRRIGATION, GL-LAW)
DRY-03 [research] A dryback is not a drought. Moderate, timed deficit raised cannabinoids without yield loss in trials; severe or prolonged deficit cut both. Water before any wilt. (CAPLAN2019, STACK2024, GL-COCO)
DRY-04 [grower-practice] The rescue floor is a safety limit, not a steering target. Establish the crop's minimum moisture before using any dryback target; no dryback may cross it. In rockwool, below about 25-30% true water content the fibre stops rewetting from the dripper and only a hand-soak recovers it. The engine's P3 emergency floor is this limit. With Auto Setpoints on, code shifts it with field capacity, down as well as up (CT-12), so it holds the crop's minimum only while the probe's peaks are sound (PRB-14). (GL-ROCKWOOL, GL-SLAB, ENGINE, CTRL)
DRY-05 [vendor] Drying overnight lets air back into the root zone. When the day's watering ends sets its size: watering on late makes it smaller (a vegetative push), stopping earlier makes it larger (a generative push). (VND-IRRIGATION, GL-SLAB, GL-ROCKWOOL)
DRY-06 [grower-practice] Some dryback is essential, but deeper is not always better: a slightly wetter overnight lifted medicinal yield in a reported trial. (GL-LAW)
DRY-07 [grower-practice] Move the overnight dryback in small steps, one notch a day across a week. (GL-LAW, SKILL-PLAYBOOK)
DRY-08 [grower-practice] A dryback smaller than the probe's error (about 1-2 points calibrated, about 3 uncalibrated) is guesswork: calibrate or steer a bigger dryback. (GL-LAW, GL-PROBE, DIELECTRIC2024)
DRY-09 [grower-practice] A dryback that suddenly deepens usually means a missed shot or a demand change (light, VPD, CO2, airflow). Check the shot log and climate first; add maintenance water only once controller and probe are verified. (GL-SLAB, GL-ROCKWOOL)
DRY-10 [product-rule] A zone whose P0 ends on its timeout is not stuck: whenever dryback_target is larger than the morning drop, P0 waits for its timeout (or the bypass) unless the dawn call starts the ramp sooner (ACT-02). Changing dryback_target or the P0 wait is the grower's setting. (ENGINE, CTRL, INTEG, BRAIN, SKILL-PLAYBOOK)
DRY-11 [product-rule] In this software the overnight dryback is set by when the day's watering stops and how wet P2 leaves the zone at that point. The brain's levers are the dusk call (end P2 in the last 3 hours, never for a zone steered vegetative, never within 3 points of the rescue floor) and the re-water threshold notch. The engine's own predictive P3 cannot pass with a dark period of 12 hours or more, nor when reaching dryback_target would take more than 12 hours, so P2 then waters until lights-off. (ENGINE, BRAIN)
### P2 maintenance
MNT-01 [grower-practice] P2 replaces the water used through the light period. Its drainage, once the substrate is at field capacity, is what carries salt out; judge it by pore EC and runoff, not by the ramp. (GL-SLAB, GL-ROCKWOOL, VND-IRRIGATION)
MNT-02 [grower-practice] Hold the zone in its band through P2, topping up when it dips; steer with EC and timing rather than swinging the moisture. (GL-LAW)
MNT-03 [vendor] Runoff at field capacity is the salt flush and the EC dipstick: about 10-20% of water fed in general, or 8-16% for vegetative and 1-7% for generative steering by vendor guidance. Catch it at representative plants; never infer it from pump run time. (VND-IRRIGATION, GL-ROCKWOOL, GL-SLAB)
MNT-04 [vendor] A consistently wetter daytime through most of flower gave more yield at the same potency in a substrate vendor's trial: steer with timing and dryback, not by starving the day. (VND-SLAB-TRIAL, GL-ROCKWOOL)
MNT-05 [grower-practice] The minimum daily volume is a safety floor that does not wait for a low reading (only the anti-drown ceiling and the p2_min_interval_min gap after the last shot hold it back), not a target. It exists only where the install configured it: the integration has no setting for it (it is a per-plant helper made by hand, times the plant count), so a fresh install runs without it, and the controller switches it off for a zone whose table is backing up. (GL-ROCKWOOL, ENGINE, CTRL, INTEG)
MNT-06 [grower-practice] A healthy moisture trace is a sawtooth: a sharp rise at each shot, a gradual fall between. A flat or only-falling line, or a shot with no step, means water did not arrive (clog, pump, line): investigate and alert, do not retune. (GL-LAW, GL-LOOP)
MNT-07 [grower-practice] Zones or slabs behaving unevenly across a room point to emitter flow or placement: catch-test near, middle and far, flush the lines, match drippers to the substrate. (GL-ROCKWOOL)
MNT-08 [vendor] As plants grow and drink faster, maintenance water has to rise to keep the dryback in range. (VND-IRRIGATION)
### Pore EC
EC-01 [grower-practice] Steer on pore EC in the substrate, not on dripper or drain EC. (GL-ROCKWOOL)
EC-02 [research] Pore EC rises through every dryback as water leaves and salt stays (in rockwool a feed near 3 can read above 5 by late afternoon; coco's exchange capacity softens the climb), and each shot pulls it back toward feed EC. (GL-LAW, HILHORST2000)
EC-03 [product-rule] Feed passing through the slab pulls pore EC toward the feed's EC. It brings pore EC down only when the feed is weaker than the water in the slab and the slab drains. The engine's EC corrections require exactly that (feed EC below pore EC, zone not backed up). (GL-ROCKWOOL, VND-IRRIGATION, ENGINE)
EC-04 [vendor] Pore EC answers the maintenance shot size through the drain water: a larger P2 shot carries more water out of the slab and brings EC down, a smaller one carries less, so salt stays and EC climbs (stacking). (VND-IRRIGATION)
EC-05 [vendor] Where the daily peak sits sets the runoff: aim it above field capacity (but below saturation) to drive drainage, at or under field capacity to hold drainage back. A peak above saturation is unreachable. With Auto Setpoints on, field capacity is the zone's own peak (CT-12), so judge this by the runoff, not by the field capacity number. (VND-IRRIGATION, CTRL)
EC-06 [grower-practice] One high reading is noise; a rise running for days past the zone's normal range is salt creep, visible days before tip burn: ease the dryback or increase runoff. (GL-LOOP)
EC-07 [product-rule] Root-zone salt is the slowest balance in the room, taking hours to settle, so judge an EC move hours later. The controller holds a settled pore-EC reading, taken at least 45 minutes after the last shot, and when it supplies one every EC rule in the engine uses it (and an EC correction then waits for the next settled reading); without one the engine falls back to the raw pore-EC reading. (GL-LOOP, ENGINE)
EC-08 [research] Compare EC only by the same method at similar moisture. Pore EC is a model estimate (off by up to about a fifth) and is invalid in nearly dry substrate. A pore EC under the feed's own EC straight after a flush with that feed cannot be real, so discard it as a sensor error. (GL-PROBE, GL-ROCKWOOL, HILHORST2000)
EC-09 [grower-practice] Pore or runoff EC still climbing right after water has gone through means the salt front is still passing: flush more (when the feed is dilutive), rather than blaming the probe. (GL-ROCKWOOL)
EC-10 [grower-practice] Pore EC below target means over-flushing: trim maintenance shots or the peak slightly and watch a full day. (GL-SLAB)
EC-11 [grower-practice] Runoff EC well above feed EC means salt is building in the substrate: more runoff or a lower feed EC. (GL-LAW)
EC-12 [grower-practice] Pore EC drifting lower over several days on an unchanged feed means the plants eat faster than they are fed; feed strength may come up a little (advice for a person). (GL-LAW, GL-COCO)
EC-13 [grower-practice] When EC leaves its stage band, check feed EC and climate before adding water, then make one recorded change. (GL-SLAB)
EC-14 [grower-practice] The fast daily EC lever is water timing (the P2 EC target, which moves the threshold); feed EC is the slow lever, changed rarely and deliberately. (SKILL-CONCEPTS)
EC-15 [product-rule] Engine mechanics: with EC stacking on, every 30 minutes in P2 the controller shifts the P2 threshold toward +1 point when settled EC is over 110% of the P2 target (water sooner) or -1 point when under 90% (dry further), within +/-20% of the base threshold; an optional PID does the same job. With stacking off there is no threshold shift. Either way, P2 fires a dilute shot when EC exceeds 1.2x target (feed dilutive, interval passed), and ec_adjust scales every ramp and top-up shot by EC. To change the operating EC, move the EC target (a person's setting), not the threshold. (CTRL, ENGINE)
EC-16 [product-rule] A zone held at maximum EC with a non-dilutive feed or an undrained table is fixed by the feed or the drainage, never by a setpoint. (ENGINE, SKILL-PLAYBOOK)
### Probes and hardware evidence
PRB-01 [grower-practice] A working probe answers each shot with a sharp rise then a slow decay; the response flattens near saturation. (GL-PROBE)
PRB-02 [product-rule] A reading pinned at zero or full scale, unchanged for a long spell, missing, stale or jittering is a probe fault: stop acting on it. The controller itself sets aside only a reading that is missing, unreadable, out of range or not updated for 20 minutes, and then copies a healthy sibling's shots or waters on a timer within the daily budget. That 20-minute test reads Home Assistant's last-updated time, which an exactly repeated value does not move, so a truly frozen reading is usually set aside after 20 minutes. A value that keeps changing a little while stuck at a wrong level, or a sensor that forces an update on every report, passes the check: name it as a suspected probe fault for a person. (GL-PROBE, CTRL, OSS-CTRL)
PRB-03 [research] The probe is one noisy local witness: it senses roughly a litre around its rods, reads to a decimal but is right to only about 1-2 points when calibrated to the substrate and about 3 when not. Channeling, dry pockets or poor contact make a sound probe unrepresentative. (GL-PROBE, GL-LAW, DIELECTRIC2024)
PRB-04 [grower-practice] A probe that differs from identically treated neighbours points to a blocked dripper or a dud probe; wetting and drying paths that diverge point to channeling. (GL-PROBE)
PRB-05 [grower-practice] Moisture that rises and falls with the substrate's daily temperature cycle is a contact or calibration artefact, not water moving. (GL-PROBE)
PRB-06 [vendor] Seat the probe tightly and leave it in one place, in a plant typical of the zone rather than one by a fan, a wall or a walkway; in a big zone, average several probes. (GL-PROBE, VND-IRRIGATION)
PRB-07 [grower-practice] An uncalibrated probe whose saturation peak sits well below the substrate's known full mark is under-reading: judge it against its own peak and trends, and steer on the shape and slope of the trace, which an offset does not change. (GL-LAW, GL-PROBE)
PRB-08 [grower-practice] One probe is a noisy witness: before acting on a trend it shows, look for a second sign that agrees (runoff starting, substrate weight, the sibling zones). The engine waters on the zone's VWC reading alone, and a probe stuck high silences the P2 top-ups and the watchdog (both need a reading under the P2 threshold). P1 still fires on its timer, so such a zone gets at least one shot a grow-day (more when p1_min_shots is set) before P2. The minimum daily floor would still water, but only where the install configured one: it is off on a fresh install and off for a backed-up table. So on a default install, past the morning ramp, nothing waters a zone whose probe keeps reporting a high, slightly varying value (an exactly frozen one is set aside after 20 minutes, PRB-02): weigh that pattern heavily, say the probe is not tracking when the shots show it (ACT-06), and raise it for a person. (GL-LAW, ENGINE, CTRL, INTEG, BRAIN)
PRB-09 [grower-practice] Check a doubtful probe in order: hardware fault, then temperature tracking, then drift against siblings; only then treat it as noisy but usable. (GL-PROBE)
PRB-10 [vendor] Caught runoff is a cross-check on the probe: its EC usually sits slightly below the pore EC the probe reports. (VND-IRRIGATION)
PRB-12 [product-rule] The engine flags a zone using under 40% of the cross-zone median daily volume (median at least 5 L) as under-drinking. Lead with investigation of probe, valve, dripper and delivery; consider a per-zone shot change only after hardware is cleared. (ENGINE, SKILL-PLAYBOOK)
PRB-13 [product-rule] Moisture rising with no water going in means the table is not draining; the engine then refuses EC corrections into that zone. (ENGINE)
PRB-14 [product-rule] With Auto Setpoints on, a zone's targets copy its probe: field capacity and the P1 target are its highest readings, and the re-water threshold and rescue floor shift with field capacity, for a zone whose probe is set aside too (CT-12). A probe drifting low (daily peaks falling over several grow-days with shots, climate and siblings unchanged) drags all four down, the rescue floor included; one drifting high ratchets them up and the zone runs wetter. Nothing in code catches this and the brain cannot move field capacity or the floor: name it as suspected probe drift for a person, with the moves the evidence shows, answer the probe question from the shots (ACT-06), and never chase it with a notch. (CTRL, BRAIN, GL-PROBE)
### Growth stage
STG-01 [grower-practice] Stage day numbers are nominal; plant signals decide. Move from setting to bulk when the vertical stretch has clearly slowed, and from bulk to finish when flower expansion slows and ripening signs dominate. (GL-SLAB)
STG-02 [grower-practice] A typical hybrid flowers 8-10 weeks and a cultivar can finish a week or more either side, so any flower-day boundary is an estimate. (GL-STAGES)
STG-03 [grower-practice] Final height is set by about flower week 4, mostly in the early stretch; most flower weight goes on in weeks 5-7. (GL-STAGES)
STG-04 [grower-practice] Feed (drip) EC and pore EC are different measurements; never compare a feed-chart EC with a root-zone EC band. (GL-STAGES)
STG-05 [vendor] Steer generative during stretch (more flower sites, tighter internodes), vegetative during bulking (bigger buds), and use the finish to bring accumulated root-zone EC down ahead of ripening. (VND-IRRIGATION)
STG-06 [product-rule] Published arcs disagree on flower weeks 4-6 (a vegetative bulk versus the deepest dryback). The install's stage-arc setting chooses (slab_default until the rockwool arc's rows carry numbers); the brain follows the selected arc and never switches arcs itself. (GL-SLAB, GL-ROCKWOOL)
STG-07 [grower-practice] Established veg is ready to flip once roots are established and uptake repeats from day to day. (GL-SLAB)
### Ripening
RIP-01 [grower-practice] Uptake falls as the plant senesces, so a modest feed-EC taper over the final week suits its appetite; that is not a flush. (GL-RIPEN)
RIP-02 [grower-practice] Long plain-water flushes showed no gain in yield, potency, terpenes or taste in blind trials and can crash pore EC and force an early fade. (GL-RIPEN)
RIP-03 [grower-practice] Flower weight keeps rising late into the cycle, so the finish must not starve the plants early. (GL-RIPEN)
RIP-04 [grower-practice] A hard, crispy fade by about flower week 6 points to over-flushing, a nitrogen crash or a pore-EC collapse: restore a modest feed. (GL-RIPEN)
RIP-05 [grower-practice] The finish's generative dryback still stops above the established minimum moisture, and goes deeper than its opening depth only on data for this cultivar. (GL-SLAB)
RIP-06 [research] Late flower humidity belongs around 45-55% with about 58% as the ceiling, judged on the worst hour (the lights-off spike), because bud rot rides the spikes and canopy air runs wetter than the room sensor. (GL-RIPEN, PUNJA2025, ZHANG2020)
RIP-07 [research] Harvest is a person's call from trichomes on mid-cola calyxes and the product objective; trichome colour is useful but subjective and cultivar-dependent, so the finish's end date stays an estimate. (GL-RIPEN, USU)
RIP-08 [grower-practice] When buds stop swelling and trichomes stall for a week, check room temperature and feed EC before deciding the plant is done. (GL-RIPEN)
RIP-09 [vendor] A vendor fade programme (switching to a fade product late at bloom EC, plain water on the last day) is the vendor's practice; the vendor itself does not claim flushing improves quality. (VND-IRRIGATION, GL-SLAB)
### Closed-loop discipline
LOOP-01 [grower-practice] Every reading is signal plus noise: smooth it and act on the trend, never on one spike. (GL-LOOP)
LOOP-02 [grower-practice] Judge a reading against limits drawn from the zone's own history. Inside them do nothing; act only on a point past a limit or a run that is clearly not random. (GL-LOOP)
LOOP-03 [grower-practice] Before reacting, confirm three things: the reading left its usual range, it stayed out longer than one reading with sensors agreeing, and you know which lever answers it. If any fails, it is noise. (GL-LOOP)
LOOP-04 [grower-practice] Steer to a band, not a single number, and make one decisive correction when the signal clearly crosses it; reacting to every wiggle makes the zone oscillate. (GL-LOOP)
LOOP-05 [grower-practice] Check the probe against a running tally of water in minus water used and drained; a lying sensor may only push toward caution, never into flooding. (GL-LAW)
LOOP-06 [grower-practice] Every lever moves more than one balance (water, salt, humidity, heat): name which balance a change moves, and which way, before making it. (GL-LOOP)
LOOP-07 [grower-practice] Fuse several signals before flagging a problem, stay quiet by default, and attach a number and the evidence to every prescribed change. (GL-LOOP)
LOOP-08 [grower-practice] Sample fast enough to see a shot's response within about half an hour, but not so fast that jitter invites over-correction. (GL-LOOP)
LOOP-09 [vendor] Each day, collect the drain water of a few typical plants in every zone just after the P2 shots, and decide the next day's adjustments from its amount, EC and pH. (VND-IRRIGATION)
LOOP-10 [vendor] Drain water a little more alkaline than the feed is normal; drain water more acidic than the feed suggests the root zone is being kept too wet. (VND-IRRIGATION)
LOOP-11 [vendor] Where you can, put each cultivar on a separate irrigation zone: cultivars dry back at different rates. (VND-IRRIGATION)
### Substrate
SUB-01 [research] Media labels (coir, peat, rockwool) do not define hydraulic behaviour: particle size, container capacity and air-filled porosity of the actual blend and container decide it. Guide numbers are starting ranges to confirm against this room's own records; never run factory defaults unchecked. (USU, GL-SLAB, GL-LAW)
SUB-02 [research] The more control a substrate gives, the less it forgives: living soil, then coco, peat, rockwool and plain water, from most to least forgiving. (GL-LAW, MALIK2025)
SUB-05 [product-rule] Shot litres = substrate litres per plant x plants x shot %; duration = shot litres / zone flow (plants x drippers per plant x L/h per dripper / 3600). Substrate must be per-plant times plant count against zone-total flow, or shots come out plant-count times too short and the pump short-cycles. Example: 6 L per plant at 3% is 0.18 L per plant, about 2.7 minutes on one 4 L/h dripper. (CTRL, SKILL-CONCEPTS)
SUB-06 [grower-practice] One dripper per plant is a single point of failure: one plant wilting while neighbours are fine means a single dripper or contact fault. Restore flow and hand-water that plant with balanced feed. (GL-SLAB)
SUB-08 [grower-practice] Warm, persistently wet substrate starves roots of oxygen; root-zone temperature (about 20-26 C) matters alongside moisture. (GL-SLAB, GL-LAW)
SUB-09 [vendor] Smaller substrate volumes dry back faster, which makes pore EC easier to steer. (VND-IRRIGATION)
### Feed and source water
FEED-01 [product-rule] The source-water gate has two halves, feed EC and feed pH, and each runs only when its feed sensor is configured and its band is set. The feed sensors default to empty, so neither half runs on a fresh install; the pH band ships set, the EC band does not. A running half holds every shot while the feed is out of band, and holds once its probe has been dead past the grace window (correct, fail-closed). External holds (dosing, fill, flush) apply only where the install configured them. Without a live or recent feed EC reading, nothing checks the feed's strength and the engine's dilutive test (feed EC below pore EC) uses an assumed feed EC of 3.0, not a measurement: treat the feed as unchecked and any dilutive judgement, flush or block as unverified, and say so rather than acting on it. A feed EC figure in the evidence may be that assumed 3.0, not a reading, unless the evidence marks it measured. A held zone with rising pore EC is waiting on the feed, not mistuned. (CTRL, ENGINE, SKILL-CONCEPTS)
FEED-02 [research] EC measures ionic load, not full water quality: uncharged compounds escape it, and alkalinity (bicarbonate) can dominate pH behaviour. (USU)
FEED-03 [research] Softening that swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium makes water worse for plants; verify reverse-osmosis output rather than assuming it. (USU)
FEED-04 [research] Visual deficiency symptoms are late and non-specific; confirm root-zone pH and EC (and tissue tests where possible) before blaming the feed. (USU)
FEED-05 [research] A stable nutrient strategy can beat stage-by-stage recipe churn, but tank mixing, injector behaviour, short low-flow pulses and micronutrient segregation can defeat delivery. (USU)
FEED-06 [research] Dose automation must cap every pulse with a limit that does not depend on the pH or EC probe, and needs calibration, mixing delay and a check after each dose; that is code's job, not a setpoint. (USU)
### Climate
CLI-01 [grower-practice] Transpiration, driven by VPD, light and temperature, sets how fast a zone drinks. A zone drying fast or stacking EC may have a climate cause: name it alongside any irrigation compensation. (SKILL-CONCEPTS, GL-COCO)
CLI-02 [research] Plants respond to vapour pressure deficit, not relative humidity, and leaf temperature sets the real deficit: well-watered leaves sit within about 2 C of air (LED canopies usually cooler, so wetter than an air-basis chart). A leaf running hot means transpiration has stopped: check the root zone first. (NELSON2015, GROSSIORD2020, GL-VPD)
CLI-03 [vendor] Working leaf-VPD bands by convention: clones 0.4-0.8, early veg 0.8-1.1, late veg 0.9-1.2, early-mid flower 1.1-1.4, late flower 1.2-1.5 kPa. Flowering far too wet cost most of the flower biomass in one controlled trial. (VND-VPD, JIN2019, CORREDOR2025, GL-VPD)
CLI-04 [research] Stability beats precision: a steady moderate VPD outperforms chasing an ideal value through swings. (INOUE2021, GL-VPD)
CLI-05 [grower-practice] Wilting is usually a supply problem (root zone too dry or drowned), not a demand problem: check substrate moisture before raising humidity. (GL-VPD, NELSON2015)
CLI-06 [research] Photoperiod response is cultivar-dependent, and cannabis is very sensitive to light leaks during the dark period. (USU)
CLI-07 [research] CO2 response is coupled to light, temperature and room leakage; preventing below-ambient depletion can matter more than a generic enrichment target. (USU)

### Brain contract (observer -> planner -> guarded controller -> executor -> audit)
BRN-01 [product-rule] Architecture: an observer estimates plant state from proxy streams and never actuates; a plan or recipe declares targets; a deterministic guarded controller applies freshness, confidence, permission, phase, range and physical-safety gates; an executor runs idempotent commands with read-back; an audit trail records estimate, request, gate result, command and measured result. (PSO)
BRN-02 [product-rule] The model selects within a finite action set that code generates and may explain its choice; it never invents setpoints, edits an approved plan or recipe, or bypasses a gate. An independent validator still decides whether the pick executes. (PSO)
BRN-03 [product-rule] Missing, stale or invalid data blocks the brain's own autonomous actions: fail closed. The controller's water-safety paths are separate: a zone with a dead or set-aside probe still waters, by copying a sibling or on a timer (CT-5). (PSO, CTRL)
BRN-04 [product-rule] Approved plan revisions are immutable. The brain's phase calls, EC steer modes and probe set-asides are directives that expire on their own; a setpoint notch is not. It is written to the zone's own number and stays there: only the rescue-shot revert (BR-6), a person's edit or an Auto Setpoints move replaces it. Never notch again on the belief that an earlier notch has lapsed. (PSO, BRAIN)
BRN-05 [product-rule] VWC, EC, VPD, light and runoff are proxies for plant state, not measurements of it; every estimate should carry confidence, freshness and what is missing. (PSO)
BRN-06 [product-rule] A notification or chat reply is not an approval. (PSO)
BRN-07 [product-rule] In journal text, call each phase by the words the zone's status tile already shows the grower (listed under the engine helpers: zone_status_label), so the journal and the dashboard agree. Internal keys stay P0-P3. (PSO, ENGINE)
